La Ciencia

Miopía cognitiva — Cuando estás excitado, la corteza prefrontal es anulada por el sistema límbico. Estudios demuestran que se toman decisiones drásticamente más riesgosas. No es debilidad; es neurología.

La brecha empática frío-caliente explica por qué es casi imposible predecir tu comportamiento cuando estás excitado si estás en un estado de calma. Escáneres cerebrales revelan que la excitación sexual activa los mismos circuitos de recompensa que las drogas: tu cerebro literalmente no puede evaluar las consecuencias de la misma manera. Por eso las pre-decisiones importan: define tus límites antes de estar en un estado alterado.

3 Deepfakes y Contenido Generado por IA

Amenaza Emergente

Los deepfakes son videos o imágenes creados con inteligencia artificial que parecen reales pero no lo son. Los depredadores los usan para crear contenido íntimo falso con tu rostro y amenazarte con publicarlo.

  • Qué son: Videos o imágenes generados por IA que superponen tu rostro en contenido existente. No necesitan fotos íntimas reales — una sola foto de perfil puede ser suficiente para crear contenido falso convincente.
  • Cómo se usan en sextorsión: Crean deepfakes usando fotos de tus redes sociales y te amenazan con publicarlos si no pagas. También clonan tu voz para hacer llamadas falsas a familiares.
  • Cómo detectarlos: Pelo irrevelante o borroso, ojos que no parpadean o tienen color inconsistente, movimientos extraños en el cuello, calidad inconsistente entre rostro y fondo, fundidos o transiciones antinaturales.
  • Cómo protegerse: Reduce fotos públicas con el rostro visible, no compartas fotos con iluminación frontal, usa marcas de agua en fotos personales, verifica identidad en videollamadas en vivo.

Marco legal: El TAKE IT DOWN Act (EE.UU., 2025) penaliza con hasta 2 años por compartir deepfakes y 18 meses por amenazar con ellos. Canadá (Bill C-16, 2026) aumentó la pena máxima a 10 años. Reino Unido (Crime and Policing Act 2026) penaliza crear o proveer generadores de deepfakes con hasta 3 años. La UE (Directiva 2024/1385) obliga a criminalizar deepfakes por 2027.

6 Antecedentes Legales

Leyes contra el Abuso Sexual Digital

El abuso sexual basado en imágenes está tipificado como delito en la mayoría de jurisdicciones del mundo. El panorama legal ha evolucionado rápidamente, con muchos países aprobando leyes específicas en lugar de basarse en estatutos generales. Leyes clave:

  • Estados Unidos — Take It Down Act (2025): 2–3 años para adultos, hasta 3 años para menores. Ciberacoso federal (18 USC 2261A): hasta 5 años. La mayoría de procesos por sextorsión resultan en 5–20 años según los cargos. La FTC permite reclamar daños civiles.
  • Reino Unido — Online Safety Act 2023: hasta 5 años por compartir imágenes íntimas sin consentimiento. Ofcom puede multar a plataformas hasta £18 millones o 10% de ingresos globales.
  • Canadá — Código Penal s.162.1: hasta 5 años por distribución no consentida. Código Penal s.264.2: hasta 10 años por acoso criminal.
  • España — Art. 197.7 CP: prisión de 3 meses a 1 año, o multa de 6–12 meses por difusión no consentida. Si las imágenes se obtuvieron sin consentimiento: 1–4 años. Con menores o fines lucrativos: hasta 7 años.
  • Italia — Codice Rosso (Legge 69/2019) art. 612-ter: 1–6 años de prisión por difusión no consentida de imágenes íntimas. Legge 134/2021 aumentó las penas por sextorsión y ciberacoso.
  • México — Ley Olimpia (federal + todos los estados): 3–6 años más multa de 500–1,000 UMA. Algunos estados (Nuevo León, Jalisco) imponen hasta 12 años.
  • Argentina — Ley Belén (27.736): 6 meses a 2 años por difusión de imágenes íntimas. Amenazas: 3 meses a 1 año. Extorsión (art. 169 CP): 3–8 años.
  • Chile — Art. 161-B CP: prisión por extorsión con imágenes íntimas. Art. 161-C: grabación/difusión de imágenes íntimas en lugares públicos. Ley 21.675 amplió las protecciones.
  • Colombia — Ley 2064 (proyecto): 4–6 años más multa de 50–100 SMLMV. Art. 244 CP (extorsión): 16–24 años.
  • Perú — Art. 153 CP (extorsión): 10–15 años, hasta cadena perpetua con agravantes. Ley 31814 amplió protecciones contra violencia digital.
  • Internacional — El Convenio de Estambul (Consejo de Europa) y el Convenio de Budapest sobre Ciberdelincuencia ofrecen marcos transfronterizos para el enjuiciamiento y la asistencia legal mutua.
  • Deepfakes — TAKE IT DOWN Act (EE.UU., 2025): hasta 2 años por compartir deepfakes, 18 meses por amenazar con ellos. Bill C-16 (Canadá, 2026): máxima de 10 años. Crime and Policing Act 2026 (RU): hasta 3 años por crear/proveer generadores. Directiva UE 2024/1385: debe criminalizarse para 2027. 61 reguladores globales emitieron declaración conjunta (2026).

Tus derechos: Las víctimas tienen derecho a presentar denuncias penales, solicitar la eliminación del contenido, obtener órdenes de restricción y reclamar daños civiles. Muchos países ofrecen opciones de denuncia anónima y procesos de eliminación expeditados. La ley está de tu lado.

Importante: Las leyes varían según la jurisdicción. Si eres víctima, contacta a un abogado local o una organización de apoyo para conocer las protecciones específicas en tu país o estado.

Don't Decide While Aroused

Sexual arousal hijacks rational judgment. Predators know this — and they weaponize it against you.

The Science

Cognitive myopia — When sexually aroused, your prefrontal cortex (rational decision-making) is suppressed by the limbic system. Studies show people under arousal make drastically riskier choices, even when they know better. It's not weakness; it's neurology.

The hot-cold empathy gap explains why it's nearly impossible to predict your behavior while aroused when you're in a calm state. Brain scans reveal that sexual arousal activates the same reward circuits as substance cravings — your brain literally cannot weigh consequences the same way. This is why pre-decisions matter: decide your boundaries before you're in a heightened state.

1 What You Risk

Sextortion

Blackmail where perpetrators threaten to distribute intimate images unless demands are met.

300%+ FBI case increase (2021-2024). Perpetrators pose as partners on dating apps, escalate to explicit exchanges, then threaten. Most operate from West Africa, Eastern Europe, and Southeast Asia. Teens and young men are the most targeted demographic. The FBI reported over $100M in losses in 2023 alone. Perpetrators often work in organized rings sharing scripts and victim lists.

Cyber Scams & Phishing

Fraud using sexual content as bait to steal money or sensitive data.

Variants: Sugar daddy/momma scams, fake webcam shows, romance scams, sexting bots that harvest contacts. Social engineering: Scammers use psychological triggers like loneliness, desire for intimacy, or financial desperation. They often build rapport for weeks before asking for money or explicit content. AI-generated profile photos make detection increasingly difficult. Red flags: Refuses video calls, professes love unusually fast, always has an excuse not to meet in person.

Online Extortion

Demanding payment through threats to release sensitive information.

Amplified by AI: Bots scrape data, clone voices, generate deepfake pornography to threaten victims with content that was never real. Multi-platform attacks: Perpetrators gather information from dating apps, social media, and data breaches to build comprehensive profiles. They often combine sextortion with credential theft — demanding both money and access to accounts. The escalation pattern: Initial demand is small (to test compliance), then grows exponentially once a victim pays.

Reputation Damage

Intimate content online is permanent. It resurfaces years later.

During:
  • Job background checks
  • College admissions
  • Child custody proceedings
  • Political campaigns
  • Professional licensing reviews
Even "disappearing" messages can be screenshotted or recovered. Digital permanence: Once shared, content can be copied, downloaded, uploaded, and distributed across the internet within minutes. Removal is extremely difficult — content often persists on obscure forums even after takedown. The Streisand effect: Attempting to hide content can sometimes draw more attention to it.

Financial Ruin

Victims lose thousands on average. Perpetrators exploit the shame cycle repeatedly.

Repeat victimization: Shame of the first payment makes reporting feel impossible, so perpetrators drain victims again and again. Average losses: The FBI reports average individual losses of $3,000-$7,500 in sextortion cases. Some victims lose their entire life savings. Cryptocurrency demands: Perpetrators increasingly demand payment in Bitcoin or gift cards, making transactions nearly untraceable and unrecoverable. Secondary scams: After paying, victims are often targeted again by the same or different perpetrators who buy victim lists.

Psychological Trauma

Victims suffer anxiety, depression, and PTSD from sustained harassment and fear of exposure.

Chronic stress: The ongoing threat creates hypervigilance, sleep disorders, and social withdrawal. Many victims report the psychological damage outlasts the extortion itself. Suicide risk: Studies show sextortion victims are significantly more likely to experience suicidal ideation. The combination of shame, fear, and perceived isolation is devastating. Recovery: Professional counseling, support groups, and victim advocacy programs are critical for recovery. The psychological impact should not be underestimated — and it is never the victim's fault.

Doxing & Identity Theft

Personal information leaked publicly, identities stolen, accounts hijacked across platforms.

Total exposure: Perpetrators compile real names, addresses, employers, and family members from multiple breaches. Once doxed, the data spreads across forums and is rarely fully removed.

Relationship Sabotage

Perpetrators send content to partners, family members, or employers to destroy support networks.

Strategic isolation: By targeting the people closest to you, perpetrators eliminate your support system and increase your dependency on complying with their demands.

Non-Consensual Sharing

Sharing intimate content without consent is illegal in many jurisdictions. Perpetrators distribute private images to humiliate, control, or exploit victims.

Image-based abuse: Even content originally shared consensually can be weaponized later. Laws in the US, UK, EU, and Latin America now criminalize non-consensual distribution. Victim rights include takedown orders and civil damages. You never owe anyone an intimate image — and sharing it without consent is a crime.

Consensual Sharing Risks

Even when both parties consent, intimate content can leak through hacks, device theft, or changing trust dynamics. Once sent, you lose control.

Permanent loss of control: Screenshots, cloud breaches, malware, and ex-partners are all vectors. Over 90% of non-consensual distribution cases start with content originally shared consensually. The safest intimate media is the one never captured.

◆ The Predator's Playbook

1

Initial Contact

A predator creates a fake profile on social media or dating apps, then initiates conversation. Profiles are carefully crafted to appear attractive and trustworthy — often using AI-generated or stolen photos.

2

Building Trust (Grooming)

Rapport is built over days or weeks using love bombing, empathy, and shared interests. They move you to private platforms like WhatsApp, Telegram, or Snapchat where content is harder to trace.

3

Raising the Temperature

The conversation turns sexual. They ask for intimate photos or videos — sometimes sending theirs first to pressure reciprocity. This is the critical moment where rational judgment is most compromised by arousal.

4

The Trap Closes

Once they have your content, threats begin. They demand money, more content, or both — threatening to expose you to family, friends, and employers. A countdown or deadline creates false urgency.

5

Exploitation or Escape

If you pay, demands escalate and your info is sold to other criminals. This cycle only ends when you block, report, and seek help. The shame is engineered — it is never your fault.

3 Deepfakes & AI-Generated Content

Emerging Threat

Deepfakes are videos or images created with artificial intelligence that look real but aren't. Predators use them to create fake intimate content with your face and threaten to publish it.

  • What they are: AI-generated videos or images that superimpose your face onto existing content. They don't need real intimate photos — a single profile picture can be enough to create convincing fake content.
  • How they're used in sextortion: They create deepfakes using your social media photos and threaten to publish them unless you pay. They also clone your voice for fake calls to family members.
  • How to detect them: Stray or blurry hair, eyes that don't blink or have inconsistent color, strange neck movements, inconsistent quality between face and background, unnatural fades or transitions.
  • How to protect yourself: Reduce public photos with your face visible, don't share photos with frontal lighting, use watermarks on personal photos, verify identity in live video calls.

Legal framework: The TAKE IT DOWN Act (US, 2025) carries up to 2 years for sharing deepfakes and 18 months for threatening with them. Canada (Bill C-16, 2026) increased the maximum penalty to 10 years. UK (Crime and Policing Act 2026) criminalizes creating or supplying deepfake generators with up to 3 years. EU (Directive 2024/1385) requires criminalizing deepfakes by 2027.

4 How to Protect Yourself

  • Pause If aroused or emotionally heightened, do not make financial decisions or share media.
  • Stay anonymous Keep face, tattoos, and identifiable backgrounds out of intimate media.
  • Segment accounts Never link dating profiles to your real social media or phone number.
  • Verify Reverse image search profile photos. Scammers use stolen pictures.
  • Paying is futile Extortionists rarely delete material — they sell or reuse it.
  • Report & block Do not negotiate. Report to local police and the platform immediately.
  • Get support Cyber Civil Rights Initiative and Thorn offer free help for victims.
  • Encrypt everything Use Signal or Wickr for sensitive conversations. Enable disappearing messages.
  • Freeze your credit Place a credit freeze to prevent perpetrators from opening accounts in your name.
  • Know the laws Many countries criminalize non-consensual image sharing. Document everything.
  • Staged identity Keep dating profiles separate from your real identity: separate email, phone, social media.
  • Trust your gut If something feels off, it probably is. Don't ignore red flags for politeness.

5 If It Happens to You

  1. Stop all communication with the perpetrator.
  2. Do not pay. Paying funds the crime and rarely stops the threats.
  3. Save evidence — screenshots, URLs, profiles, messages.
  4. Report at cybertipline.org or ic3.gov (FBI).
  5. Contact a local victim advocacy group.
  6. Talk to someone you trust. Isolation is the extortionist's greatest weapon.

6 Legal Background

Laws Against Image-Based Abuse

Image-based sexual abuse is now criminalized in most jurisdictions worldwide. The legal landscape has evolved rapidly, with many countries enacting specific laws rather than relying on general statutes. Key laws include:

  • United States — The Take It Down Act (2025) carries 2–3 years for adults and up to 3 years for minors who sextort victims. Federal cyberstalking (18 USC 2261A) carries up to 5 years. Most sextortion prosecutions yield 5–20 years depending on charges. The FTC also allows victims to seek civil damages.
  • United Kingdom — Online Safety Act 2023: up to 5 years for sharing intimate images without consent. Ofcom can fine platforms up to £18 million or 10% of global revenue.
  • Canada — Criminal Code s.162.1: up to 5 years for non-consensual distribution. Criminal Code s.264.2: up to 10 years for criminal harassment.
  • Spain — Art. 197.7 CP: 3 months to 1 year in prison, or a fine of 6–12 months for non-consensual sharing. If images are obtained without consent: 1–4 years. With minors or for profit: up to 7 years.
  • Italy — Codice Rosso (Legge 69/2019) art. 612-ter: 1–6 years for non-consensual distribution of intimate images. Legge 134/2021 increased penalties for sextortion and cyberstalking.
  • Mexico — Ley Olimpia (federal + all states): 3–6 years plus fine of 500–1,000 UMA. Some states (e.g., Nuevo León, Jalisco) impose up to 12 years.
  • Argentina — Ley Belén (27.736): 6 months to 2 years for diffusion of intimate images. Threats: 3 months to 1 year. Extortion (art. 169 CP): 3–8 years.
  • Chile — Art. 161-B CP: imprisonment for extortion using intimate images. Art. 161-C: recording/diffusing intimate images in public places. Ley 21.675 expanded protections.
  • Colombia — Ley 2064 (project): 4–6 years plus fine of 50–100 SMLMV. Art. 244 CP (extortion): 16–24 years.
  • Peru — Art. 153 CP (extortion): 10–15 years, up to life imprisonment with aggravating factors. Law 31814 expanded digital violence protections.
  • Brazil — Art. 158 CP (extortion): 4–10 years plus fine. PL 2058/2024 (sextortion specific): 5–12 years plus fine. Art. 218-C CP (revenge porn): existing penalties apply.
  • European Union — Directive 2024/1385 on violence against women requires all member states to criminalize non-consensual sharing, cyberstalking, and online harassment. Member states must transpose this into national law by 2027.

O abuso sexual baseado em imagens é criminalizado na maioria das jurisdições do mundo. O panorama jurídico evoluiu rapidamente, com muitos países aprovando leis específicas em vez de depender de estatutos gerais. Leis principais:

  • Chile — Art. 161-B CP: prisão por extorsão com imagens íntimas. Art. 161-C: gravação/difusão de imagens íntimas em locais públicos. Lei 21.675 expandiu proteções.
  • Colômbia — Lei 2064 (projeto): 4–6 anos mais multa de 50–100 SMLMV. Art. 244 CP (extorsão): 16–24 anos.
  • Peru — Art. 153 CP (extorsão): 10–15 anos, até prisão perpétua com agravantes. Lei 31814 expandiu proteções contra violência digital.
  • Brasil — Art. 158 CP (extorsão): 4–10 anos mais multa. PL 2058/2024 (sextorsão específica): 5–12 anos mais multa. Art. 218-C CP (revenge porn): penas existentes se aplicam.

  • Deepfakes — TAKE IT DOWN Act (EUA, 2025): até 2 anos por compartilhar deepfakes, 18 meses por ameaçar com eles. Bill C-16 (Canadá, 2026): máxima de 10 anos. Crime and Policing Act 2026 (RU): até 3 anos por criar/fornecer geradores. Diretiva UE 2024/1385: deve ser criminalizado até 2027. 61 reguladores globais emitiram declaração conjunta (2026).

La conversazione diventa sessuale. Chiede foto o video intimi — a volte inviando i suoi prima per fare pressione con reciprocità. Questo è il momento critico in cui l'eccitazione compromette maggiormente il giudizio razionale.

4

La Trappola si Chiude

Una volta che hanno il tuo contenuto, iniziano le minacce. Chiedono denaro, più contenuti o entrambi — minacciando di esporti a famiglia, amici e datori di lavoro. Un conto alla rovescia crea falsa urgenza.

5

Sfruttamento o Fuga

Se paghi, le richieste aumentano e le tue informazioni vengono vendute ad altri criminali. Questo ciclo termina solo quando blocchi, denunci e cerchi aiuto. La vergogna è artificiosa — non è mai colpa tua.

3 Deepfake e Contenuto Generato dall'IA

Minaccia Emergente

I deepfake sono video o immagini creati con intelligenza artificiale che sembrano reali ma non lo sono. I predatori li usano per creare contenuti intimi falsi con il tuo volto e minacciare di pubblicarli.

  • Cosa sono: Video o immagini generate dall'IA che sovrappongono il tuo volto su contenuti esistenti. Non hanno bisogno di foto intime reali — una singola foto del profilo può essere sufficiente.
  • Come vengono usati nella sextorsione: Creano deepfake usando le tue foto dei social media e minacciano di pubblicarli se non paghi. Clonano anche la tua voce per chiamate false ai familiari.
  • Come riconoscerli: Capelli vaganti o sfocati, occhi che non battono o hanno colore inconsistente, movimenti strani del collo, qualità inconsistente tra volto e sfondo.
  • Come proteggersi: Riduci le foto pubbliche con il volto visibile, non condividere foto con illuminazione frontale, usa filigrane sulle foto personali, verifica l'identità nelle videochiamate in live.

Quadro normativo: Il TAKE IT DOWN Act (USA, 2025) prevede fino a 2 anni per condividere deepfake e 18 mesi per minacciarli. Canada (Bill C-16, 2026) ha aumentato la pena massima a 10 anni. Regno Unito (Crime and Policing Act 2026) criminalizza creare o fornire generatori di deepfake con fino a 3 anni. UE (Direttiva 2024/1385) richiede di criminalizzare i deepfake entro il 2027.

4 Come Proteggerti

  • Fai una pausa Se sei eccitato o alterato, non prendere decisioni finanziarie né condividere contenuti.
  • Nascondi il viso Tieni viso, tatuaggi e sfondi riconoscibili fuori dai contenuti intimi.
  • Account separati Non collegare i profili di appuntamenti ai tuoi social reali.
  • Verifica Ricerca inversa delle foto profilo. I truffatori usano immagini rubate.
  • Pagare è inutile Raramente eliminano il materiale — lo vendono o lo riusano.
  • Denuncia e blocca Non negoziare. Segnala alla polizia e alla piattaforma.
  • Chiedi supporto Cyber Civil Rights Initiative e Thorn offrono aiuto gratuito.
  • Crittografa tutto Usa Signal o Wickr. Attiva i messaggi che si autodistruggono.
  • Congela il credito Impedisci che aprano conti a tuo nome.
  • Conosci le leggi Molti paesi penalizzano la condivisione non consensuale di immagini intime.
  • Identità separata Usa email, telefono e social diversi per gli appuntamenti.
  • Fidati del tuo istinto Se qualcosa sembra sbagliato, probabilmente lo è.

5 Se Ti Succede

  1. Interrompi ogni comunicazione con il criminale.
  2. Non pagare. Pagare finanzia il crimine e raramente ferma le minacce.
  3. Salva le prove — screenshot, URL, profili, messaggi.
  4. Denuncia su cybertipline.org o alla Polizia Postale.
  5. Contatta un gruppo locale di supporto alle vittime.
  6. Parla con qualcuno di cui ti fidi. L'isolamento è l'arma più potente del ricattatore.

6 Contesto Legale

Leggi contro l'Abuso Sessuale Digitale

L'abuso sessuale basato su immagini è penalizzato nella maggior parte delle giurisdizioni del mondo. Il panorama legale si è evoluto rapidamente, con molti paesi che hanno approvato leggi specifiche. Leggi principali:

  • Italia — Codice Rosso (Legge 69/2019) art. 612-ter: 1–6 anni di reclusione per la diffusione non consensuale di immagini o video intimi. Legge 134/2021 ha inasprito le pene per sextortion e cyberstalking. La Polizia Postale dispone di unità specializzate per i crimini informatici.
  • Stati Uniti — Take It Down Act (2025): 2–3 anni per adulti, fino a 3 anni per minorenni. Cyberstalking federale (18 USC 2261A): fino a 5 anni. La maggior parte dei processi per sextortion prevede 5–20 anni a seconda dei capi d'accusa.
  • Regno Unito — Online Safety Act 2023: fino a 5 anni per la condivisione di immagini intime senza consenso. Ofcom può multare le piattaforme fino a £18 milioni o il 10% del fatturato globale.
  • Spagna — Art. 197.7 CP: reclusione da 3 mesi a 1 anno, o multa da 6–12 mesi per la diffusione non consensuale. Se le immagini sono state ottenute senza consenso: 1–4 anni. Con minorenni o a fini di lucro: fino a 7 anni.
  • Messico — Legge Olimpia (federale + tutti gli stati): 3–6 anni più multa di 500–1,000 UMA. Alcuni stati (Nuevo León, Jalisco) prevedono fino a 12 anni.
  • Argentina — Legge Belén (27.736): 6 mesi a 2 anni per la diffusione di immagini intime. Minacce: 3 mesi a 1 anno. Estorsione (art. 169 CP): 3–8 anni.
  • Cile — Art. 161-B CP: reclusione per estorsione con immagini intime. Art. 161-C: registrazione/diffusione di immagini intime in luoghi pubblici. Legge 21.675 ha ampliato le protezioni.
  • Colombia — Legge 2064 (progetto): 4–6 anni più multa di 50–100 SMLMV. Art. 244 CP (estorsione): 16–24 anni.
  • Perù — Art. 153 CP (estorsione): 10–15 anni, fino all'ergastolo con aggravanti. Legge 31814 ha ampliato le protezioni contro la violenza digitale.
  • America Latina — Legge Olimpia (Messico), Legge Belén (Argentina, Legge 27.736) e leggi simili in Cile (Legge 21.675), Colombia (Legge 2064) e Perù (Legge 31814) penalizzano specificamente la violenza sessuale digitale.
  • Unione Europea — Direttiva 2024/1385 sulla violenza contro le donne richiede a tutti gli stati membri di penalizzare la diffusione non consensuale, il cyberstalking e le molestie online. Recepimento obbligatorio entro il 2027.
  • Deepfake — TAKE IT DOWN Act (USA, 2025): fino a 2 anni per condividere deepfake, 18 mesi per minacciarli. Bill C-16 (Canada, 2026): massima di 10 anni. Crime and Policing Act 2026 (UK): fino a 3 anni per creare/fornire generatori. Direttiva UE 2024/1385: deve essere criminalizzato entro il 2027. 61 regolatori globali hanno emesso dichiarazione congiunta (2026).

I tuoi diritti: Le vittime hanno diritto a sporgere denuncia penale, richiedere la rimozione dei contenuti, ottenere misure cautelari e chiedere il risarcimento dei danni civili. Molti paesi offrono opzioni di denuncia anonima e processi di rimozione accelerata. La legge è dalla tua parte.

Importante: Le leggi variano in base alla giurisdizione. Se sei una vittima, contatta un avvocato locale o un'organizzazione di supporto per conoscere le tutele specifiche nel tuo paese o stato.
